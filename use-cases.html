Use cases

When a heavy delivery needs a lift.

From a truck with no usable liftgate to a warehouse short on capacity, start with the job you need done.

Find your kind of lift

These are examples of jobs that may call for a forklift, an operator, or both. The equipment class depends on the actual load, lift height, route, surface, and site rules.

Use cases

No dock or insufficient liftgate

01

Unload a crated CNC machine

A machine shop receiving a crated CNC unit needs the truck-to-ground move and an unobstructed route to a staging point. Share the crate weight, footprint, truck type, and placement location.

02

Unload an oversized generator

A generator may exceed the delivery truck’s liftgate rating. Provide the shipping weight, skid dimensions, unloading side, and ground conditions.

03

Receive a commercial HVAC unit

At a building without a loading dock, the first lift can be the hardest. Note whether the unit arrives on a flatbed or enclosed trailer and where it must be set.

04

Unload a transformer or switchgear cabinet

Electrical equipment may have a high center of gravity and specific handling instructions. Share the rigging points, crate dimensions, and manufacturer restrictions.

05

Unload commercial kitchen equipment

Restaurants and commissaries often lack truck-level receiving. Specify door widths, thresholds, and whether the job ends at curbside or inside placement.

06

Unload a safe or vault component

Heavy concentrated loads require attention to weight, floor limits, and the route. Include the manufacturer’s handling guidance and final staging point.

07

Receive heavy medical or laboratory equipment

Installation crews may need a machine for truck unloading and staging. Provide packaging size, receiving access, and any site-specific delivery rules.

08

Recover a delivery that cannot be unloaded

If a truck arrives and the consignee has no suitable dock or forklift, share the carrier’s waiting window, truck configuration, load weight, and site access immediately.

Use cases

Construction and outdoor materials

09

Unload palletized building materials

A jobsite flatbed delivery may need a forklift to take materials off the truck and stage them near the work. Note pallet weights and terrain.

10

Receive solar panels or battery cabinets

Commercial solar and electrical projects may bring heavy pallets or cabinets. Include package weights, unloading location, and any restricted handling.

11

Unload stone, tile, or countertop crates

Dense material can exceed a small liftgate’s capacity. Share the crate weight, fork access, and whether the surface is paved or uneven.

12

Unload hardscape or landscaping pallets

Pavers, block, and soil pallets often arrive where no dock exists. Tell us the truck position and whether the forklift must travel over gravel or grass.

13

Place materials around a construction site

The job may include unloading and short-distance distribution. Identify drop points, grades, gates, and overhead obstructions.

14

Unload retail fixtures for a store opening

A new store may have limited receiving access. Provide fixture packaging, lift weights, delivery restrictions, and staging space.

Use cases

Machinery and plant moves

15

Receive equipment before the site has a forklift

A new business may need temporary unloading capacity before permanent equipment arrives. Tell us the heaviest item and site readiness.

16

Move a delivered machine into its installation area

Beyond unloading, a machine may need to travel from a staging point toward installation. Detail the route, floor, doorway widths, and placement requirements.

17

Unload a pump, compressor, or motor

Plant repairs often involve a single urgent heavy component. Provide weight, lifting points, delivery vehicle, and the safe staging area.

18

Unload a printing press or production machine

Equipment dealers and print shops may need a controlled truck-to-floor transfer. Share the crate or machine footprint and access constraints.

19

Rearrange a factory or warehouse layout

A temporary forklift and operator may help relocate equipment during a layout change. Describe each move and the travel path.

20

Load sold machinery onto an outbound truck

Sellers or auction buyers may need loading where no suitable forklift is on site. Provide the machine’s weight, packaging, and truck deck height.

Use cases

Warehouses, events, and temporary work

21

Unload pallets at a pop-up warehouse

Temporary distribution space may have no equipment on site. Estimate pallet count, maximum pallet weight, arrival timing, and aisle layout.

22

Unload a trailer when the forklift is unavailable

A broken or undersized site forklift can block receiving. Include the trailer type, load size, floor surface, and urgency.

23

Handle inbound freight during a warehouse move

A surge or inventory reset may require extra lifting capacity for a defined window. Share expected pallet count and shift hours.

24

Unload trade-show or production crates

Venue load-in can be time constrained and access controlled. Provide crate weights, dock or ramp details, check-in rules, and move-in schedule.

25

Move heavy equipment into or out of storage

A temporary storage move may need a forklift at one or both ends. Detail truck access, unit door widths, and the load path.

Not sure what kind of forklift fits?

Tell us the load weight and dimensions, where it starts and ends, the surface, access width, and the date. LiftCrew can review the scope and suggest a forklift class; equipment and availability are confirmed before scheduling.
